# Turning a CISSP practice result into a better decision

Allow 180 minutes for the 100-item form. This is fixed-length practice, not a reproduction of adaptive exam scoring. A practice percentage is not the official scaled score.

For each missed or uncertain question, write four short observations before reading the explanation:

1. **Required outcome:** What must the business or affected people actually be able to do? A running application may not have correct balances; a signed attendance sheet may not establish recovery capacity.
2. **Current evidence:** What is established, and what is only proposed? Distinguish a genuine provider report from a report covering this deployment, and successful patch installation from a repeated authorization test.
3. **Authority and timing:** Who can accept this risk or approve this action? Is the question before design, during an incident, or after an emergency change? The best action at one stage may be inadequate at another.
4. **Cost of the tempting alternative:** Identify the specific business dependency, permission boundary, unsupported claim, or avoidable delay it would introduce. Do not write only “less secure.”

## Worked reasoning patterns, without a one-word answer rule

- **A treatment has a business cost.** Compare actual exposure and feasible options with the owner. This is not a rule to hold a meeting before every action: an active loss and an already authorized containment plan can justify immediate scoped action.
- **A control passed its own test.** Ask whether that test establishes the claimed end-to-end outcome. Reuse valid evidence; do not discard every earlier result merely because a new gap exists.
- **A normal process is unavailable.** Look for an authorized bounded alternative that still achieves its protection objective. Urgency neither grants unlimited authority nor proves that the only safe choice is to stop all work.
- **A supplier promises protection.** Distinguish evidence of capability from allocation of financial consequences. A warranty cannot make a suspect device authentic, but it may still be useful in an overall agreement.
- **Data or identities cross a boundary.** Authentication, encryption, purpose approval, resource authorization and field minimization answer different questions. Determine which one actually failed.
- **A proposed improvement makes something worse.** Preserve the real requirement while testing alternatives: key separation must still support recovery; a network restriction must preserve necessary clinical traffic; a controlled exit must remain usable under the supplied safety plan.

## A useful error log

Record the question ID, your selected action, the missed scenario fact, the decisive tradeoff, and what evidence would make your alternative reasonable. Group errors by reasoning failure, not only by domain. A pattern such as “accepted a process metric as an outcome” can appear in operations, audit, recovery and development.

Use the review edition's per-option references for targeted reading. ISC2's outline defines exam scope. The NIST, IETF and first-party engineering references explain specific principles; their control names are not a requirement to memorize a private course's answer labels. Practice recognizing the same principle in a genuinely different business decision.
